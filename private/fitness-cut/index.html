The Cut — Lose Fat, Keep the Muscle

A 90-day fat-loss plan built for one job: strip 25–40 lbs while protecting every ounce of muscle definition you already have. Strength is not the goal here — muscle retention is. That means you still lift with intent (heavy-ish, low volume) so your body has a reason to hold onto lean mass, while the deficit and cardio do the fat-burning. Every workout comes in two versions: Gym and Home. Pick whichever fits the day.

🧮
Let's be honest about the timeline. 30–40 lbs in 12 weeks is 2.5–3.3 lbs/week — aggressive, and above the rate that reliably preserves muscle. The safe, muscle-sparing sweet spot is 1.5–2.5 lbs/week. Realistically that lands you at ~208–218 lbs by day 90, with the full 30–40 lbs arriving around weeks 14–18. The good news: at 238 lbs your first 2–3 weeks will drop fast (water + glycogen), so early progress will feel dramatic. Aim for the process, not the scale number.

Why this keeps muscle

💪
In a calorie deficit your body will happily burn muscle unless you give it two reasons not to: (1) a mechanical signal — lifting with real load, even briefly — and (2) enough protein. Nail those two and the fat comes off while your definition stays. That's the entire strategy. Cardio and steps just widen the deficit.

The Three Phases

Weeks 1–4

Ignite. Set the deficit, build the habit, hit your step target daily. Full-body lifting 3×/wk to lock in the muscle-retention signal. Fast early scale drops.

Weeks 5–8

Grind. Add a 4th lifting day and one interval session. This is where discipline beats motivation — the scale slows, the mirror keeps improving.

Weeks 9–12

Sharpen. Cardio volume peaks, lifting stays heavy-ish to defend muscle. Definition becomes visible. Optional short "diet break" if energy tanks.

1.5–2.5 lbs/wk

Fat-loss target. Fast enough to see real change, slow enough to hold muscle and avoid rebound. Adjust food, not effort, if you stall.

Four resistance days (each has a gym and a home version), two dedicated cardio days, one full rest day. Every day — training or not — you hit your step target.

Guiding Principles

Non-negotiables
1

Protein first, every day — ~190–215g (roughly 0.8–0.9g per lb). This is the single biggest lever for keeping definition in a cut.

2

Lift heavy-ish, keep it short — 6–12 reps, 1–2 reps in reserve. You are defending muscle, not chasing PRs. Don't drop the weights just because you're dieting.

3

Hit your steps — work up to 10,000–12,000 steps/day. Walking burns real calories, protects your knees at 238 lbs, and never eats into recovery.

4

Deficit, not starvation — cut ~500–750 cal/day below maintenance. Crash diets torch muscle and rebound. Slower loss = definition kept.

5

Sleep 7–8 hours — short sleep raises cortisol and cravings and accelerates muscle loss in a deficit. Recovery is where the fat-burning consolidates.

6

Weigh weekly, not daily — track the 7-day average. Day-to-day water swings will lie to you. Adjust food only if the average stalls for 10+ days.

📏
Track the mirror and the tape, not just the scale. As you keep muscle and lose fat, the scale can stall for a week while your waist keeps shrinking. Measure your waist at the navel every 2 weeks — it's the truest read on a cut.

Two full-body sessions, alternated. Each hits legs, push, pull, and core so you train everything twice a week — the most efficient way to hold muscle while cutting. Rest 60–90 sec between sets. To save time and bump the calorie burn, pair the two exercises marked (superset) back-to-back.

Cardio here is a tool to widen the deficit, not the main event. Two guardrails at 238 lbs: keep most of it low-impact to protect your knees, and don't let it be so brutal it wrecks recovery from lifting (which is what actually holds your muscle). Steps do the quiet heavy lifting — they burn more total weekly calories than your workouts and cost you nothing in recovery.

Dedicated cardio days (Tue / Thu)

  • Weeks 1–4 — steady state: 30–40 min exercise bike, incline treadmill walk, or brisk outdoor walk. Conversational pace, RPE 5–6 / 10.
  • Weeks 5–8 — add intervals (1 session/wk): bike or rower — 1 min hard / 2 min easy × 8 rounds. Keep the second cardio day as steady state.
  • Weeks 9–12 — peak: one HIIT session (30 sec hard / 90 sec easy × 8–10) + one 40–50 min steady-state session.
  • Low-impact first: bike, rower, incline walk, elliptical — save running until you're lighter and only if the knees are happy.
❤️
Fat-burning zone myth, briefly: low intensity burns a higher percentage of fat but fewer total calories; hard intervals burn more total. Both work — total weekly calories burned is what matters. Use steady state to protect recovery and intervals when you're short on time.
⚠️
Don't out-cardio your recovery. If your lifts start dropping fast, you feel wiped, or sleep suffers, pull back the intervals before you touch the lifting. Losing the muscle-retention stimulus is the one mistake that ruins a cut — you'd lose the definition you're working to keep.

Greek yogurt, a protein shake, cottage cheese, or a protein bar (20g+ protein, under 200 cal). Pair with fruit or a rice cake if training soon. ~30g protein.

6–8 oz salmon, lean beef, chicken, or shrimp + a large portion of vegetables (broccoli, asparagus, zucchini, peppers, spinach) + a small starch. On rest days, drop the starch. ~50g protein.

Cottage cheese or casein shake — slow-digesting protein that supports overnight muscle repair. Keep it under 200 cal.

Alcohol is the fastest way to stall this cut. Empty calories, worse sleep, a blunted fat-burn the next day, and it wrecks your food discipline. You don't have to ban it — but every drink is a direct tax on your results. Cutting it is the single easiest win here.

Simple habits that move the needle

  • Protein at every meal — it's the most filling macro and the one that saves your muscle. Hit it before you worry about anything else.
  • Drink water before meals — 80–100+ oz/day. Thirst masquerades as hunger constantly.
  • Volume eating — vegetables and lean protein fill you up for very few calories. Bulk every plate with them.
  • Stop drinking your calories — soda, juice, sugary coffee, and beer add up fast and never fill you up.
  • Plan one flexible meal a week — a planned meal out keeps you sane and consistent. It's the daily unplanned grazing that derails cuts, not one dinner.
  • Consider creatine (5g/day) — one of the most evidence-backed supplements; helps preserve strength and muscle while dieting. Clear it with your doctor first.
